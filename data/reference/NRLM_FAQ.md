# NRLM (DAY-NRLM) in Meghalaya — Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. What is NRLM?**
NRLM (National Rural Livelihoods Mission, also called Aajeevika and now DAY-NRLM) is the Government of India's flagship programme to reduce rural poverty. It organises poor rural households, mainly women, into Self Help Groups (SHGs), federates them into Village Organisations (VOs) and Cluster Level Federations (CLFs), and links them to community funds, bank credit, skills training and livelihood support.

**2. Who implements NRLM in Meghalaya?**
The Meghalaya State Rural Livelihoods Society (MSRLS), the nodal agency under the Community & Rural Development (C&RD) Department, Government of Meghalaya. It works through a State Mission Management Unit in Shillong, District Mission Management Units (DMMUs) and Block Mission Management Units (BMMUs).

**3. When was NRLM launched?**
The national mission was launched by the Ministry of Rural Development in June 2011, replacing the older SGSY scheme. MSRLS was created in 2012 to implement it in Meghalaya, and field work began in the state in 2014–15.

**4. What is the full form of DAY-NRLM?**
Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission. It is the current name of NRLM/Aajeevika.

**5. What is a Self Help Group (SHG)?**
An SHG is a voluntary, affinity-based group of poor rural households, usually women, who meet regularly, save together and lend to each other. National norms say 10–20 members; in Meghalaya the Chief Minister has described SHGs as voluntary groups of 10–15 women that do not require formal registration. Groups in remote tribal or difficult areas, and groups of persons with disabilities, can be as small as 5 members.

**6. What are VOs and CLFs?**
A Village Organisation (VO) is a federation of SHGs in a village. A Cluster Level Federation (CLF) is a federation of VOs across a cluster of villages. In Meghalaya, CLFs are being registered under the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015.

**7. What are the "Panchasutra"?**
The five principles every SHG is expected to follow: regular meetings, regular savings, regular internal lending, timely repayment, and up-to-date books of accounts.

**8. Who is eligible to join?**
Poor rural households identified through the Participatory Identification of the Poor (PIP) process, in which the community identifies poor households and the Gram Sabha vets the list. NRLM starts with women as representatives of their households; persons with disabilities, the elderly and single women/widows are prioritised, and men can join special SHGs for persons with disabilities, elders and other special categories.

**9. Is NRLM only for women?**
No, but women are the main focus. For especially vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities and the elderly, both men and women are mobilised. Interest subvention on bank loans applies to women SHGs.

**10. Does NRLM give cash directly to individuals?**
No. NRLM does not provide direct financial support to individual members. Money goes to community institutions (SHGs, VOs, CLFs) as Revolving Fund, Community Investment Fund and Vulnerability Reduction Fund. Members benefit through loans from their SHG and from banks linked to the SHG.

**11. What is the Revolving Fund (RF)?**
Seed capital given to an SHG that is at least 3 or 6 months old, follows the Panchasutra, and has not received RF before. The amount is ₹10,000 minimum and up to ₹15,000 per SHG. It is used for internal lending, to strengthen the group's financial management and to build a credit history.

**12. What is the Community Investment Fund (CIF)?**
A fund given to the CLF (about ₹3,000 per SHG member in intensive blocks) and kept as a perpetual resource to lend to SHGs or to fund collective activities. Until a CLF exists, an SHG can receive up to ₹50,000 directly against its micro-plan. The MSRLS 2022–23 report also mentions a plan to release CIF at ₹1,10,000 per SHG through CLFs. Exact amounts can change, so confirm with the BMMU.

**13. What is the Vulnerability Reduction Fund (VRF)?**
About ₹1,500 per member, given to Village Organisations in intensive blocks to address vulnerabilities such as food security, health security and the needs of vulnerable people in the village.

**14. What is interest subvention?**
A provision where NRLM covers the difference between the bank's lending rate and 7% on bank credit taken by women SHGs. The MSRLS FAQ states a ceiling of ₹3 lakh per SHG; national rules have been revised since, so check with the bank or BMMU for the current limit.

**15. How much bank loan can an SHG get?**
RBI guidelines allow collateral-free loans to SHGs under DAY-NRLM up to ₹20 lakh, and no lien is marked on the SHG's savings account. A Lakhpati Didi provision allows one woman per SHG to receive a loan of up to ₹1 lakh. Actual sanction depends on the bank's assessment of the SHG.

**16. What are the eligibility conditions for an SHG to get a bank loan?**
All members should be at least 18 years old; the SHG should have been functioning for at least 6 months as per its books of accounts (not from the date of opening the bank account); and it should follow the Panchasutra.

**17. How do I join or form an SHG?**
Contact your Block Mission Management Unit (BMMU) at the C&RD Block office, or approach an existing SHG or Village Organisation in your village. MSRLS staff and community resource persons (CRPs) help left-out households form or join groups, and train them on meetings, savings, bookkeeping and financial literacy.

**18. What documents are required?**
There is no fixed document list for joining an SHG, since groups are voluntary and most documents are needed at the bank stage.

*To open the SHG savings bank account:*
- Account opening form
- Resolution of the SHG authorising the account and naming the authorised office bearers
- KYC (identity and address proof) of the office bearers. If the bank does e-KYC, Aadhaar alone is enough.
- PAN is not required (Form 60 is used where needed), and KYC of all members is not required.

*To apply for a bank loan:*
- Signed loan application and the SHG's resolution/loan authorisation letter
- Inter-se agreement signed by all members
- SHG records (savings, loan, minutes, internal lending and attendance registers)
- Micro-credit plan appraised by the federation or support agency (usually from the third linkage onward)
- Recommendation from the mission/support agency, KYC of members and passport-size photographs
- No "no dues" certificate is needed, and not every member needs a personal savings account at the time of credit linkage.

*For individual members:* Aadhaar and proof of village residence are generally expected, plus a bank account (the SHG cadre can help open one). Confirm with your BMMU.

*For skills training (Aajeevika Skills):* proof of age (18–35), identity proof, and proof of belonging to a rural poor household (PIP listing, BPL status or Gram Panchayat certificate).

**19. Are there any fees to join an SHG?**
NRLM does not charge a joining fee. Members contribute regular savings to their own group (for example, one SHG in the MSRLS annual report saves a small fixed amount weekly), and that money stays with the group and its members.

**20. What is a micro-plan and why is it needed?**
A micro-plan (micro-credit or livelihood plan) is prepared by the SHG's members to assess their resources, needs and plans. SHGs receive CIF only after preparing it, and from the third bank linkage onward banks usually ask for it as well.

**21. What other support does NRLM offer apart from money?**
Training in SHG management and bookkeeping, financial literacy, bank linkage through Bank Sakhis, insurance, farm livelihoods support (MKSP, agri-nutri gardens, livestock care through Pashu Sakhis), non-farm enterprise support (SVEP, market linkage), skill training (Aajeevika Skills, RSETI), and social development work on nutrition, health, gender and social inclusion.

**22. What is the Lakhpati Didi initiative?**
An initiative under DAY-NRLM that helps SHG women take up multiple livelihood activities and earn at least ₹1 lakh a year on a sustainable basis (an average of about ₹10,000 a month). It focuses on individual SHG women, supported by their SHGs, VOs and CLFs. In February 2026 the Chief Minister reported that 41,323 SHG members in Meghalaya had achieved "lakhpati" status.

**23. What are RSETIs?**
Rural Self Employment Training Institutes are bank-run institutes that train rural youth for self-employment and link them to bank loans. Meghalaya has five: SBI (Umran, Ri Bhoi), SBI Tura, Meghalaya Rural Bank Nongstoin, PNB Mawphlang and MCAB Williamnagar.

**24. How widely has NRLM reached in Meghalaya?**
By March 2023, MSRLS reported coverage of all 12 districts and 46 blocks, about 4.27 lakh households in 44,037 SHGs, 4,598 VOs and 68 CLFs, and roughly ₹288 crore of community funds released. In February 2026 the Chief Minister said SHGs cover nearly 90% of households, with over 58,000 groups having access to about ₹1,100 crore, around 70% of it bank credit.

**25. Who funds the programme?**
The Centre and the State share the cost on a 90:10 basis, as stated by the C&RD Department for Meghalaya.

**26. Where can I get official, up-to-date information?**
- MSRLS website: msrls.nic.in (FAQs, notices, annual reports, newsletters), email msrls.2011@gmail.com
- C&RD Department: megcnrd.gov.in
- DAY-NRLM national portal (Ministry of Rural Development)
- Your local BMMU / C&RD Block office, and the bank branch where your SHG holds its account

**27. Why might some amounts or figures differ between sources?**
Fund amounts, loan limits and interest-subvention rules are set nationally by the Ministry of Rural Development and RBI and are revised from time to time. Coverage figures in Meghalaya change each year. Some third-party guide websites quote different ranges, so MSRLS and Ministry sources should be preferred, and any time-sensitive figure should be confirmed with the BMMU.
