# PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) — Complete Scheme Reference

## 1. Overview

Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (PMAY) is the Government of India's flagship affordable housing mission, launched to achieve **"Housing for All"** by providing financial assistance, interest subsidies, and technical support to help eligible economically weaker, low-income, and middle-income families construct, purchase, or improve a permanent (pucca) home.

PMAY is implemented through **two separate but related sub-missions**:
- **PMAY-Urban (PMAY-U):** for applicants residing in urban/town areas, implemented by the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA)**.
- **PMAY-Gramin (PMAY-G):** for applicants residing in rural areas, implemented by the **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD)**.

Both sub-missions share the same overarching objective — universal access to a pucca house with basic amenities — but operate under different guidelines, eligibility frameworks, verticals, funding patterns, and monitoring systems, described separately below.

### Legal Status: Scheme vs. Statutory Right
PMAY is a **government scheme/mission implemented through executive guidelines and budgetary allocation**, not a law passed by Parliament. This is an important distinction from a rights-based law: there is **no individual legal entitlement to a house** under PMAY. Sanction of a house depends on a beneficiary being identified/approved through the scheme's eligibility and selection process (SECC/Awaas+ list and Gram Sabha approval for PMAY-G; income-category and documentation-based application for PMAY-U) and on availability of budgetary allocation, not on a court-enforceable guarantee. This differs fundamentally from statutory entitlement schemes.

### What is a "Pucca House"?
A **pucca house** is a permanent, all-weather dwelling constructed with durable materials (such as burnt brick, stone, cement, or concrete walls and roof) that can withstand normal wear, weather, and natural elements over a long period. It is contrasted with a **kutcha house**, built from temporary or non-durable materials (such as mud, thatch, unburnt brick, or bamboo), which requires frequent repair and offers little protection from weather. Under PMAY, "not owning a pucca house anywhere in India" is a core eligibility condition, since the scheme is targeted at households that currently lack adequate permanent housing; a household living in a kutcha or severely damaged house remains eligible even if they technically "own" that structure.

## 2. Launch and Legislative/Administrative Timeline

- **PMAY-Urban (PMAY-U):** Launched on **25 June 2015** by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, with an original mission period up to 31 March 2022. This was extended (without changing the funding pattern or implementation methodology) to 31 December 2025, to allow completion of houses sanctioned up to 31 March 2022.
- **PMAY-Gramin (PMAY-G):** Launched with effect from **1 April 2016**, by restructuring the earlier **Indira Awaas Yojana (IAY)** into a new, more transparent, data-driven framework, implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development. The Union Cabinet has since approved continuation of PMAY-G for the period **FY 2024-25 to FY 2028-29**, targeting an additional 2 crore houses (cumulative target of around 4.95 crore houses) to cover the Awaas+ (2018) list and remaining eligible households from the SECC 2011 Permanent Wait List.
- **PMAY-Urban 2.0 (PMAY-U 2.0):** A revamped second phase of the urban mission, approved by the Union Cabinet on **9 August 2024** (announced in the Union Budget 2024), and launched in **September 2024**, to be implemented over **5 years (1 September 2024 to 2029)**, targeting the housing needs of **1 crore additional urban families**.
- PMAY was launched under the leadership of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, in pursuance of the government's stated vision of "Housing for All."

## 3. Objectives of the Scheme

1. **"Housing for All":** The central objective is to ensure every eligible family — urban and rural — has access to a permanent, pucca house with basic amenities.
2. **Slum redevelopment and inclusion:** Addressing the housing needs of slum dwellers and other economically weaker urban residents.
3. **Affordable home ownership:** Making home ownership accessible to first-time buyers across Economically Weaker Section (EWS), Low Income Group (LIG), and Middle Income Group (MIG) categories, through interest subsidies and direct financial assistance.
4. **Basic amenities and dignified living:** Ensuring PMAY houses are converged with access to toilets, drinking water, electricity, and clean cooking fuel, not just four walls and a roof.
5. **Women's empowerment and joint ownership:** Promoting house allotment in the name of the female head of household, or joint ownership between spouses, to strengthen women's property rights.
6. **Social equity:** Prioritising SC/ST households, minorities, persons with disabilities, widows, single women, manual scavengers, and other vulnerable/underprivileged groups.
7. **Urban rental housing access:** (Introduced under PMAY-U 2.0) Creating dedicated, adequate rental housing stock for urban migrants, industrial workers, and other groups who need affordable rental accommodation rather than ownership.
8. **Technology-enabled, transparent delivery:** Using digital tools — geo-tagging, Aadhaar-based verification, real-time MIS dashboards — to ensure funds reach genuine beneficiaries and construction progress is independently verifiable.

## 4. PMAY-Urban (PMAY-U): Structure and Verticals

### 4.1 Original PMAY-U (2015 onwards) — Four Verticals
The original PMAY-U mission was implemented through four verticals:
1. **In-Situ Slum Redevelopment (ISSR):** Redeveloping slums on the land they currently occupy, using land as a resource, in partnership with private developers.
2. **Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme (CLSS):** Interest subsidy on home loans for EWS/LIG/MIG beneficiaries buying, constructing, or improving a house (valid up to 31 March 2022).
3. **Affordable Housing in Partnership (AHP):** Central assistance to public/private agencies constructing affordable houses for EWS beneficiaries.
4. **Beneficiary-Led Construction/Enhancement (BLC):** Direct central assistance to individual eligible households to construct or enhance their own house on land they already own.

### 4.2 PMAY-Urban 2.0 (from September 2024) — Revised Four Verticals
PMAY-U 2.0 restructured the mission into a new set of four verticals, dropping ISSR (found inefficient per a 17th Lok Sabha committee report) and elevating rental housing to a full vertical:
1. **Beneficiary-Led Construction (BLC):** Central assistance directly to eligible households to construct a new house or enhance an existing one on land they own.
2. **Affordable Housing in Partnership (AHP):** Affordable housing projects (30–45 sqm carpet area) developed through partnership between States/UTs, Urban Local Bodies (ULBs), and public/private agencies, allotted to EWS beneficiaries.
3. **Affordable Rental Housing (ARH):** Creation of adequate rental housing stock (up to 60 sqm carpet area) for EWS/LIG beneficiaries, including urban migrants, the homeless, industrial workers, working women, construction workers, street vendors, and similar urban-poor groups; can include conversion of vacant government-funded housing stock into rental housing.
4. **Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS):** Interest subsidy (up to ₹1.80 lakh) on home loans for EWS/LIG/MIG families for purchase, re-purchase, or construction of a house (up to 120 sqm carpet area for MIG); applicable to loans sanctioned and disbursed on or after 1 September 2024.

- PMAY-U 2.0 is implemented as a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** for BLC, AHP, and ARH, and as a **Central Sector Scheme** for the ISS vertical.
- A **beneficiary family** is defined to comprise husband, wife, and unmarried sons and/or daughters.
- Central assistance under BLC/AHP is around **₹2.50 lakh per unit** in most states, with a higher central share (around ₹2.25 lakh central + higher minimum state contribution, per specific state notifications) for North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and the UTs of Jammu & Kashmir, Puducherry, and Delhi.

## 5. PMAY-Gramin (PMAY-G): Structure

- PMAY-G's objective is to provide a pucca house with basic amenities to rural households that are **houseless** or living in **kutcha/severely damaged houses**.
- **Unit assistance:** ₹1.20 lakh per house in plain areas, and ₹1.30 lakh per house in North-Eastern and Himalayan states/hilly/difficult areas and Integrated Action Plan (IAP) districts.
- **Minimum house size:** 25 sqm, including a dedicated area for hygienic cooking (enhanced from the earlier Indira Awaas Yojana norm of 20 sqm).
- **Optional top-up loan:** Beneficiaries who choose to can avail an additional loan (facilitated up to around ₹70,000) from financial institutions to build a larger or better-finished house.
- **Toilet integration:** Construction of a toilet is a **mandatory, integral part** of the PMAY-G house, funded through convergence with the Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G) or MGNREGA; a house is treated as "complete" only once its toilet is also constructed.
- **Construction timeline:** The house is expected to be completed within **12 months** of the date of sanction.
- **National Technical Support Agency (NTSA):** Envisaged at the national level to improve the quality of construction under PMAY-G.

## 6. Eligibility Criteria

### 6.1 General/Common Conditions (both PMAY-U and PMAY-G)
- The applicant's household must **not own a pucca (permanent) house** anywhere in India, in the name of any family member.
- The household must **not have previously availed central assistance** under any housing scheme from the Government of India or a state government, and must not have already received a PMAY subsidy.

### 6.2 PMAY-Urban Income Categories
Applicants are classified into:
- **EWS (Economically Weaker Section):** household income up to a defined annual threshold (historically around ₹3 lakh).
- **LIG (Low Income Group):** household income up to a higher threshold (historically around ₹6 lakh).
- **MIG-I / MIG-II (Middle Income Group):** progressively higher household income thresholds (historically up to around ₹12 lakh and ₹18 lakh respectively).
- Because these limits are periodically revised, they should always be confirmed against the current official PMAY-U notification rather than assumed fixed.

### 6.3 PMAY-Gramin Beneficiary Identification
- Beneficiaries are identified using **Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC), 2011** data, supplemented by the subsequent **Awaas+ (2018) survey**, rather than a simple BPL/income test.
- The eligible universe includes households that are **houseless**, or living in **zero, one, or two-room kutcha houses** with kutcha walls and kutcha roof.
- Priority is assigned using objectively verifiable deprivation parameters (housing condition, social category, vulnerability indicators), not purely income.
- Selection follows a **three-step verification process**: (i) SECC/Awaas+ data-based shortlisting, (ii) validation and approval by the **Gram Sabha**, and (iii) **geo-tagging** of the house/location to confirm ground reality before sanction.
- **Objections/appeals on the beneficiary list:** The draft priority list is placed before the Gram Sabha, where any household believing it has been wrongly excluded, or any member objecting to another household's inclusion, can raise the issue for local verification and correction before the list is finalised. A household **left out of the original SECC 2011 data** can be newly covered through the **Awaas+ survey** — self-registering via the AwaasPlus app (with Aadhaar-based identity verification) so it can be considered in the next selection cycle, rather than needing to wait for another full census.
- If a household believes it has been wrongly excluded even after Gram Sabha review, it can escalate through the block/district rural development office, or file a formal grievance (see Section 12).

## 7. Application and Registration Process

### 7.1 PMAY-Urban
- Applicants can apply **online** through the official PMAY-U portal or **offline** through a Common Service Centre (CSC), by submitting the application form with Aadhaar details.
- Required documents typically include Aadhaar details, income proof, proof of not owning a pucca house, bank account details, and (depending on the vertical) property-related documents such as an allotment letter, approved building plan, or valuation certificate.

### 7.2 PMAY-Gramin
- PMAY-G does not rely on individual applications in the same way; rather, eligible households are **identified from the SECC/Awaas+ list**, verified locally, and approved by the Gram Sabha.
- At registration on the **AwaasSoft** system, the beneficiary's bank account details, nominee name, Aadhaar number, and MGNREGA job card number (where applicable) are captured.
- A geo-tagged photograph of the beneficiary in front of their current dwelling and the proposed construction site is captured using the **AwaasApp** and uploaded to AwaasSoft as part of registration.

## 8. Funding Pattern (Centre–State Cost Sharing)

### 8.1 PMAY-Gramin
- **Plain areas:** Cost shared between Centre and State in a **60:40 ratio**.
- **North-Eastern and Himalayan states** (and certain hill/difficult-area UTs): **90:10 ratio** (Centre:State).
- **Union Territories without a legislature:** **100% central funding**.
- Of the annual budgetary grant for PMAY-G, **95% is earmarked for house construction** (including a smaller allocation for administrative expenses within this), and **5% is retained at the central level as a reserve fund for special projects**.
- The annual financial allocation to states is based on the **Annual Action Plan (AAP)** approved by the **Empowered Committee**.

### 8.2 PMAY-Urban / PMAY-U 2.0
- For the **BLC, AHP, and ARH verticals**, cost is shared between the central government, state/UT governments, and (in BLC) the beneficiary, with the central government typically contributing around **₹2.50 lakh per unit** (with adjusted, often higher, central shares for North-Eastern states, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, and specific UTs).
- The **Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS)** vertical is centrally funded as a **Central Sector Scheme** (subsidy on home loans, not a construction grant).
- Funds are transferred to states/UTs and implementing agencies through the **Public Financial Management System (PFMS)**, often routed via the state's **Single Nodal Agency (SNA)** account.

## 9. Governance and Institutional Structure

### 9.1 PMAY-Urban
- **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs (MoHUA):** The nodal central ministry, responsible for policy, guidelines, and central fund release.
- **Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee (CSMC):** The apex central body that formally **approves projects** (e.g., BLC projects) submitted by states, reviews financial norms and fund disbursal processes, and monitors implementation.
- **State Level Nodal Agency (SLNA):** Designated by each state/UT government to coordinate PMAY-U implementation across the state, channel funds, and report progress to MoHUA.
- **Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) / Implementing Agencies (IAs):** Municipal corporations, municipalities, and other local bodies that directly implement projects on the ground.
- **Central Nodal Agencies (CNAs):** Designated institutions (such as specific banks/housing finance regulators) that operationalise the Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS) with Primary Lending Institutions.
- **Primary Lending Institutions (PLIs):** Banks and housing finance companies that disburse home loans and route the interest subsidy to eligible borrowers under ISS.

### 9.2 PMAY-Gramin
- **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD):** The nodal central ministry.
- **Empowered Committee:** Approves the state-wise Annual Action Plan (AAP) that determines fund allocation.
- **State Rural Development Department:** Coordinates state-level implementation.
- **Gram Panchayat / Gram Sabha:** Validates and approves the local beneficiary list drawn from SECC/Awaas+ data, and oversees ground-level implementation.
- **National Technical Support Agency (NTSA):** Provides technical support to improve construction quality nationally.

## 10. Disbursement Mechanism: Installments and Verification

- Financial assistance under PMAY (particularly the BLC vertical of PMAY-U, and PMAY-G construction assistance) is released to beneficiaries in **staged installments linked to construction progress**, rather than as a single lump sum, to ensure funds are used for actual construction.
- **Geo-tagging-linked releases:** Under PMAY-U, the **Bhuvan portal** (of the National Remote Sensing Centre) supports a **five-stage geo-tagging** framework for each beneficiary's house (from foundation to completion); a subsequent installment is released only after geo-tagged confirmation of the required construction stage for all beneficiaries in a project, with the **completion stage** required before the **final installment**.
- **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT):** Central assistance (and matching state/ULB share, where applicable) is credited **directly into the beneficiary's bank account**, based on the verified/geo-tagged construction stage, transferred through PFMS.
- **AwaasSoft/AwaasApp (PMAY-G):** The rural equivalent system used to register beneficiaries, record geo-tagged photographs of the house at each stage, and trigger installment releases as construction progresses.
- The final installment is typically withheld until the **toilet has also been constructed** (PMAY-G) or the completion stage of geo-tagging is confirmed (PMAY-U).
- **Construction completion timeline:** PMAY-G houses are expected to be completed within **12 months** of sanction (see Section 5). PMAY-U does not prescribe a single uniform national completion deadline for individual BLC/AHP houses in the same way; instead, projects are approved and monitored against **project-specific timelines set at sanction** (typically ranging from about 12 to 18 months depending on vertical and construction method), with progress tracked through the staged geo-tagging system described above, and overall mission-level completion deadlines set at the scheme level (e.g., the extended 31 December 2025 deadline for houses sanctioned under the original PMAY-U by 31 March 2022).

## 11. Monitoring, Digital Systems, and Transparency Mechanisms

- **PMAY-U MIS:** A GIS-based Management Information System tracking sanction, fund release, and construction progress, integrated with:
  - **Bhuvan Portal** (National Remote Sensing Centre) and **Bharat Map** (National Informatics Centre) for geo-tagging.
  - **CLSS Awas Portal (CLAP):** A real-time monitoring platform connecting MoHUA, Central Nodal Agencies, Primary Lending Institutions, and beneficiaries for tracking interest-subsidy applications and status, integrated with UIDAI for Aadhaar validation and de-duplication.
  - **UMANG mobile app** and the **DBT Bharat portal**, for citizen-facing status tracking.
  - **NITI Aayog Dashboard**, for high-level national progress monitoring.
  - Cross-linkage with the **PMAY-G MIS**, specifically to check for and prevent duplicate beneficiaries across the urban and rural schemes.
- **AwaasSoft (PMAY-G):** The centralised MIS for rural housing, recording beneficiary details, fund flow, and construction status at the household level.
- **Third-party quality monitoring and social audits:** Both PMAY-U and PMAY-G provide for independent, third-party monitoring of construction quality, alongside community-level social audits, with defined financial norms for states to conduct these activities.
- **Public disclosure of geo-tagged images:** Geo-tagged house images, as moderated by states/UTs, are made available in the public domain for transparency.
- **Capacity-building support:** Central assistance is also available to states for related activities such as setting up State/City Level Technical Cells (SLTC/CLTC), preparing the Housing for All Plan of Action (HFAPoA), and conducting training, workshops, and exposure visits — approved and normed by the CSMC.

## 12. Convergence with Other Government Schemes

PMAY (particularly PMAY-G) is deliberately converged with several other central schemes to deliver a complete, liveable home rather than a bare structure:
- **MGNREGA:** Provides **90–95 person-days of unskilled wage employment** to a PMAY-G beneficiary for the construction of their own house, over and above the housing unit assistance, effectively letting the beneficiary "earn" part of their own labour contribution.
- **Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G):** Funds the **mandatory toilet** (around ₹12,000) built as part of every PMAY-G house; a house is not treated as complete without it.
- **Saubhagya / rural electrification schemes:** For electricity connections to PMAY houses.
- **Ujjwala Yojana:** For clean cooking fuel (LPG) connections.
- **Drinking water and livelihood support programmes:** For piped water access and, in some cases, linked livelihood assistance for the beneficiary household.
- This convergence model means a single PMAY beneficiary may draw support from several schemes simultaneously (housing assistance, MGNREGA wage-days, SBM-G toilet funding, Saubhagya electricity, Ujjwala LPG) to reach a fully liveable home with basic amenities.

## 13. Technology Sub-Mission: GHTC-India and Light House Projects

- **Global Housing Technology Challenge – India (GHTC-India):** Launched on **14 January 2019** by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs under PMAY-U, as a **Technology Sub-Mission** to identify and mainstream innovative, sustainable, eco-friendly, and disaster-resilient construction technologies from across the globe for large-scale, faster, and cost-effective housing construction.
- **Affordable Sustainable Housing Accelerators (ASHA):** A component of GHTC-India focused on identifying and incubating promising **domestic** technologies that are not yet fully proven at scale, fostering a research and development ecosystem in the housing construction sector.
- **Light House Projects (LHPs):** Model housing projects constructed using shortlisted innovative alternate technologies best suited to the geo-climatic and hazard conditions of each region, comprising roughly 1,000 houses at each of six sites — **Indore (Madhya Pradesh), Rajkot (Gujarat), Chennai (Tamil Nadu), Ranchi (Jharkhand), Agartala (Tripura), and Lucknow (Uttar Pradesh)** — built with technical handholding from IITs. LHPs are intended to demonstrate faster, more economical, and higher-quality construction compared to conventional methods, with learnings documented and standardised for wider adoption.
- Once complete, houses built under LHPs are allotted to eligible beneficiaries following the **standard PMAY-U eligibility and allotment guidelines**, like any other PMAY-U house.

## 14. Grievance Redressal

- **PMAY-Urban:**
  - **PGRAMS (PMAY-Urban Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System):** The scheme-specific online portal for lodging complaints related to PMAY-U, including issues with the Credit Linked Subsidy Scheme (CLSS)/Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS), application status, or beneficiary disputes.
  - **MoHUA Public Grievances Cell:** The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs maintains a dedicated Public Grievances Cell (at its headquarters) that handles grievances relating to all organisations under the Ministry, including PMAY-U.
  - **CPGRAMS (Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System):** The Government of India's central, cross-ministry grievance portal (pgportal.gov.in), also accessible via a standalone mobile app and through UMANG, where a PMAY-related grievance can be lodged if it isn't resolved through scheme-specific channels; it also provides an **appeal facility** if the complainant is unsatisfied with the initial resolution.
- **PMAY-Gramin:**
  - Grievances (e.g., beneficiary list disputes, delayed Fund Transfer Orders (FTOs), missing geo-tag uploads, incorrect installment amounts) can be raised with the **Gram Panchayat** or the **Block Development Officer (BDO)** in the first instance.
  - If unresolved at the local level, complaints can be filed online through **CPGRAMS (pgportal.gov.in)**, selecting the Ministry of Rural Development and the PMAY-G scheme.
  - Common troubleshooting steps used by officials include checking the beneficiary's status and Fund Transfer Order (FTO) date on AwaasSoft, verifying whether required stage photographs/geo-tags have been uploaded, and confirming the beneficiary's Aadhaar-bank seeding status if a processed payment has not reached the account.
- Since PMAY is a scheme rather than a statutory entitlement (see Section 1), grievance redressal here functions as an **administrative correction mechanism** rather than a legally enforceable right to compensation, unlike some provisions under statutory schemes.

## 15. Penalties, Misuse, and Corrective Provisions

- **Fund misutilisation by beneficiaries:** If sanctioned housing assistance is found to be misused (e.g., diverted for a purpose other than house construction, or construction is not completed), implementing authorities can withhold subsequent installments, and in confirmed misuse cases, initiate recovery of disbursed funds through applicable state revenue recovery procedures.
- **Action against fraudulent/duplicate beneficiaries:** Cross-verification between the PMAY-U and PMAY-G MIS systems, and Aadhaar-based de-duplication (via UIDAI integration), is specifically used to detect and remove duplicate or ineligible beneficiaries from both schemes.
- **Action against non-performing or fraudulent implementing/private agencies:** Public/private agencies engaged under AHP or ARH found violating project terms, delivering substandard construction, or misusing central assistance can face contract termination, recovery of released funds, and exclusion (blacklisting) from future PMAY project partnerships, at the discretion of the sanctioning authority (CSMC/SLNA).
- **Utilisation Certificates (UCs):** States/UTs and implementing agencies are required to submit Utilisation Certificates confirming that previously released installments were spent on the sanctioned purpose, as a precondition for release of the next installment of central assistance — a routine financial-compliance safeguard used throughout both PMAY-U and PMAY-G fund flows.
- **RTI-based public accountability:** Beneficiary-list and subsidy data are considered public information; officials/citizens can seek specific verification records (e.g., an individual household's eligibility verification report) through a formal **Right to Information (RTI)** request where MIS data alone doesn't resolve a dispute.

## 16. Tax Benefits Linked to Affordable Housing (Section 80EEA)

- **Section 80EEA of the Income Tax Act, 1961** provided first-time homebuyers an **additional deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year** on home loan interest, over and above the ₹2 lakh deduction available under Section 24(b), specifically for loans taken to purchase **affordable housing** (property stamp duty value not exceeding ₹45 lakh).
- **Eligibility conditions (while the provision was open for new loans):** the taxpayer had to be an individual (not available to HUFs, firms, or companies), a first-time homebuyer with no other residential property in their name (or their spouse's) on the date of loan sanction, and had to opt for the **old tax regime**, since the deduction is not available under the new regime.
- **Current status:** The window for **loan sanction** eligible for this deduction closed on **31 March 2022** — loans sanctioned after that date are not eligible for a fresh 80EEA claim. Borrowers whose loans were sanctioned within the eligible window (1 April 2019 to 31 March 2022) can continue claiming the deduction annually for the remaining tenure of that specific loan. As of the latest available information, the provision has not been reopened for new loan sanctions; homebuyers taking a **new** loan should rely on the standard Section 24(b) and Section 80C benefits instead, and check current Union Budget announcements for any change in status.
- This tax benefit is administered by the **Income Tax Department**, separately from PMAY's own Interest Subsidy Scheme (ISS)/CLSS (which is a subsidy credited against the loan, disbursed through the lending bank) — a borrower's PMAY eligibility does not automatically determine 80EEA eligibility, and vice versa, since the two operate under different criteria and authorities.

## 17. Special Provisions for Vulnerable Groups

- **Women:** PMAY promotes house allotment in the **name of the female head of household**, or **joint ownership** between spouses, to strengthen women's property rights; special targets/reporting exist for houses sanctioned to women, including single women and widows.
- **SC/ST households, minorities, and manual scavengers:** Given explicit priority in beneficiary identification and selection under both PMAY-U and PMAY-G guidelines.
- **Persons with disabilities:** Considered a priority category for allotment and assistance under the scheme's vulnerability parameters.
- **Urban migrants, the homeless, and destitute persons:** Specifically targeted under the PMAY-U 2.0 **Affordable Rental Housing (ARH)** vertical, since they may need rental accommodation rather than ownership.
- **Slum dwellers:** A core target group of the original PMAY-U mission (via the earlier In-Situ Slum Redevelopment vertical), and continue to be a priority group for AHP/BLC-based rehabilitation under PMAY-U 2.0.

## 18. Checking Application/Beneficiary Status

- **PMAY-Urban:** Applicants can check sanction and disbursement status online via the official PMAY-U portal, the PMAY(U) mobile app, or the UMANG platform, using their application number, Aadhaar number, or registered mobile number; loan-linked ISS subsidy status can specifically be tracked via the CLSS Awas Portal (CLAP).
- **PMAY-Gramin:** Beneficiaries and officials can check status through the AwaasSoft portal/AwaasApp or by contacting the Gram Panchayat, using the registered beneficiary ID.

## 19. Complete End-to-End Process Flow

The lifecycle of a PMAY house, from identification/application to handover, broadly follows these stages (exact steps differ slightly between PMAY-U and PMAY-G):

1. **Identification/application:**
   - *PMAY-G:* Eligible households are identified from the SECC 2011/Awaas+ deprivation-based list.
   - *PMAY-U:* Applicants apply themselves online or via a Common Service Centre with Aadhaar and supporting documents, under the relevant vertical (BLC/AHP/ARH/ISS).
2. **Verification:** The local implementing body — Gram Panchayat/Gram Sabha for PMAY-G, or the Urban Local Body/SLNA for PMAY-U — verifies eligibility, income (for PMAY-U), and non-ownership of a pucca house.
3. **Registration on MIS:** Beneficiary details, bank account, Aadhaar, and (for PMAY-G) MGNREGA job card number are captured on AwaasSoft (PMAY-G) or the PMAY-U MIS.
4. **Approval and sanction:** For PMAY-G, the Gram Sabha approves the beneficiary list; for PMAY-U, the project/beneficiary is approved by the relevant authority, with larger projects going through the Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee (CSMC).
5. **Baseline geo-tagging:** A geo-tagged photograph of the beneficiary's current dwelling and proposed construction site is captured (via AwaasApp for PMAY-G, or the PMAY-U MIS/Bhuvan integration for PMAY-U) before construction begins.
6. **Staged fund release:** Financial assistance (PMAY-G) or central/state assistance (PMAY-U BLC/AHP) is released in installments, each triggered by verified, geo-tagged confirmation of the corresponding construction stage — foundation, roof-level/lintel, and so on — rather than as a lump sum. For PMAY-U's ISS vertical, the interest subsidy is instead credited against the sanctioned home loan through the lending bank/PLI.
7. **Construction and monitoring:** Progress is tracked through the multi-stage geo-tagging system, cross-checked by third-party quality monitors and, where applicable, social audits.
8. **Convergence with other schemes:** The mandatory toilet is completed (SBM-G/MGNREGA funding), and, where applicable, electricity (Saubhagya), LPG (Ujjwala), and water connections are arranged.
9. **Completion verification:** Once the final construction stage — including the toilet — is geo-tagged and verified as complete, the final installment is released.
10. **Handover and registration as completed:** The house is recorded as completed in the scheme's MIS, generally in the name of the female head of household or as joint ownership, and the beneficiary formally takes possession.
11. **Grievance redressal (if needed at any stage):** Disputes over eligibility, delayed installments, or construction issues are escalated through the Gram Panchayat/BDO or ULB/SLNA, and if unresolved, through PGRAMS or CPGRAMS (see Section 14).

## 20. Where to Find Authoritative, Current Information

- The official **PMAY-Urban portal** (pmay-urban.gov.in / pmaymis.gov.in) and the **PMAY-Gramin portal** (pmayg.nic.in), maintained respectively by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs and the Ministry of Rural Development, are the authoritative sources for current eligibility criteria, income limits, unit assistance amounts, vertical-wise guidelines, and application/beneficiary status.
- Because **income limits, unit costs, subsidy amounts, and mission timelines are periodically revised** by government notification, any time-sensitive figure should be verified against the latest official guideline rather than treated as permanently fixed.
