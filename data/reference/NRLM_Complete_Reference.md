# NRLM (DAY-NRLM — Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Rural Livelihoods Mission, "Aajeevika") in Meghalaya — Complete Scheme Reference

## 1. Overview

The National Rural Livelihoods Mission (NRLM), also called **Aajeevika** and, since its renaming, **DAY-NRLM**, is the Government of India's flagship poverty-reduction programme for rural areas. It works by organising poor rural households — mainly women — into **Self Help Groups (SHGs)**, federating those groups into **Village Organisations (VOs)** and **Cluster Level Federations (CLFs)**, and then connecting them to community funds, bank credit, skills training and livelihood support.

In Meghalaya, NRLM is implemented by the **Meghalaya State Rural Livelihoods Society (MSRLS)**, the nodal agency under the **Community & Rural Development (C&RD) Department**, Government of Meghalaya. MSRLS runs the programme in "mission mode" through a State Mission Management Unit (SMMU) in Shillong, District Mission Management Units (DMMUs) and Block Mission Management Units (BMMUs).

**Mission statement (as quoted in the MSRLS annual report):** to reduce poverty by enabling poor households to access gainful self-employment and skilled wage employment, resulting in appreciable improvement in their livelihoods on a sustainable basis, through building strong grassroots institutions of the poor.

**Important distinction for users:** NRLM does **not** give cash grants or subsidies directly to individual members. Financial support goes to community institutions (SHGs, VOs, CLFs) as Revolving Fund, Community Investment Fund and Vulnerability Reduction Fund, and members then access money through their SHG (internal lending) and through bank loans linked to the SHG.

**Three pillars of NRLM:** universal social mobilisation, financial inclusion, and livelihoods enhancement.

## 2. Naming and Timeline

- **2011 (June):** NRLM was launched by the Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD), Government of India, as a restructured version of the earlier Swarnajayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana (SGSY). It shifted from an "allocation-based" to a "demand-driven" approach, letting states prepare their own plans, and replaced BPL lists with **Participatory Identification of the Poor (PIP)** to identify target households.
- **2012:** The Government of Meghalaya created MSRLS as an autonomous society to implement NRLM in the state. It is registered under the Meghalaya Society Registration Act XII of 1983.
- **2014–15:** Field implementation in Meghalaya began (3 districts and 4 blocks in the first year, per the MSRLS annual report), and expanded year by year.
- **Later:** The mission was renamed **DAY-NRLM** (Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–NRLM).
- **July 2020 and July 2021:** MSRLS ran statewide PIP exercises to identify poor, extremely poor, and vulnerable households (elderly, persons with disabilities, single women/widows) left out of SHGs.
- **FY 2022–23:** Coverage reached all 12 districts and 46 blocks of the state; the federation tier (CLFs) started to be formed at scale, with 68 CLFs formed cumulatively by March 2023.
- **August 2025:** Examples of newer activity include Livelihood Service Centres and Custom Hiring Centres being inaugurated by CLFs with BMMUs (e.g., at Janepih village, Mawshynrut block).
- **February 2026:** The Chief Minister reported that SHG coverage had reached nearly 90% of households in the state (see Section 8).

## 3. Objectives of the Mission

1. **Social mobilisation:** Bring at least one member (preferably a woman) from every poor household into an SHG.
2. **Institution building:** Build SHGs, Village Organisations and Cluster Level Federations that are community-managed, accountable and self-sustaining.
3. **Financial inclusion:** Make the poor preferred clients of the banking system — through SHG bank accounts, savings, credit linkage, insurance and pension, and digital banking via Bank Sakhis.
4. **Livelihoods enhancement:** Support farm and non-farm livelihoods, enterprise development, and skilling so that households can earn sustainably (the "Lakhpati Didi" goal of ₹1 lakh or more per year).
5. **Inclusion of the most vulnerable:** Prioritise the poorest, single women, persons with disabilities, and the elderly, including through special SHGs.
6. **Social development layering:** Add focus on food, nutrition, health and WASH (FNHW), gender, social inclusion, and convergence between Panchayati Raj/traditional institutions and community organisations.
7. **Convergence:** Link community institutions with line departments and other schemes (for example MGNREGS, agriculture, horticulture, animal husbandry, NABARD, SIDBI).

## 4. Key Structural Features

- **Community institution ladder:** SHG → Village Organisation (VO) → Cluster Level Federation (CLF). In Meghalaya, CLFs are being registered under the Meghalaya Cooperative Societies Act, 2015.
- **SHG size:** Affinity-based groups of **10–20 members** under national norms. The Chief Minister has described Meghalaya SHGs as voluntary groups of **10–15 women** that do not require formal registration. Groups in remote tribal or difficult areas, and special groups of persons with disabilities, can be as small as **5 members**.
- **Women-first, but not only women:** NRLM begins with women as representatives of their households. For persons with disabilities, the elderly and other special categories, both men and women can be members of SHGs.
- **Panchasutra (five principles):** regular meetings, regular savings, regular internal lending, timely repayment, and up-to-date books of accounts. SHGs must follow these to qualify for community funds and bank credit.
- **Community cadres:** Trained community resource persons (CRPs), Master Book Keepers (MBKs), Bank Sakhis, Krishi Sakhis, Pashu Sakhis, Community Gender & Health Activists (CGHAs) and others support the groups on the ground. The MSRLS 2022–23 report records 8,637 community cadres.
- **Grading and audit:** SHGs are graded once they are 3 months old and then quarterly, and internal audits are done twice a year by MBKs.
- **Funding pattern:** Centre and State share costs on a **90:10** basis for Meghalaya (as stated by the C&RD Department).
- **Implementation units:** SMMU (State), DMMU (District), BMMU (Block), each with dedicated mission staff.
- **Digital systems:** The NRLM MIS portal tracks SHGs and funds; the LokOS app and CLF web application are being rolled out for CLF record-keeping.

## 5. Eligibility Criteria

- **Target households:** NRLM Target Households (NTH) are identified through **Participatory Identification of the Poor (PIP)** — a community-driven process in which community organisations identify poor households using participatory tools, and the list is then vetted by the Gram Sabha. NRLM does not use the BPL list for this.
- **Rural residents:** NRLM is a rural programme; poor rural households are the target. In Meghalaya, the PIP exercise of July 2020 identified about **4.29 lakh** poor and poorest-of-the-poor households as the NRLM target group.
- **Who can join an SHG:** At least one member, preferably a woman, from each poor household. Special SHGs exist for persons with disabilities, the elderly and single women/widows, who are prioritised.
- **To receive Revolving Fund:** The SHG should have existed for a minimum of 3 or 6 months, follow the Panchasutra, and not have received a Revolving Fund before.
- **To receive bank loans (national criteria):** All members should be at least 18 years old at the time of applying; the SHG should have been actively functioning for at least 6 months as per its books of accounts (not counted from the date of opening the bank account); and it should follow the Panchasutra.
- **For skills training (Aajeevika Skills/ASDP):** Trainees aged 18–35 from a rural poor household identified through PIP; until PIP is complete, BPL status or a Gram Panchayat certificate showing poor background has been accepted.
- **Interest subvention:** Applies to **women SHGs** under NRLM.

## 6. How to Join / Process

1. **Mobilisation:** MSRLS staff, community cadres (CRPs) and existing VOs identify left-out poor households in a village and help them form an SHG. Households can approach the BMMU at their C&RD Block, or an existing SHG/VO in their village, to be included.
2. **SHG formation and training:** The group elects office bearers (typically President, Secretary and Treasurer/leader), starts weekly or regular meetings and savings, appoints a bookkeeper, and receives basic training on SHG concepts, bookkeeping and financial literacy.
3. **Bank account:** The SHG opens a savings bank account (see Section 7 for documents).
4. **Grading:** At around 3 months, the SHG is graded by the VO or an MBK. Groups that pass are eligible for the Revolving Fund (and the Start-up Fund, where applicable).
5. **Federation:** The SHG joins its Village Organisation, and VOs are federated into a CLF.
6. **Micro-plan:** The SHG prepares a micro-plan (micro-credit/livelihood plan); community investment funds are released only against a micro-plan.
7. **Community funds:** Revolving Fund (to SHG), Community Investment Fund and Vulnerability Reduction Fund (via VO/CLF) are released against grading, plans and compliance.
8. **Bank credit:** The SHG applies to a bank branch for a loan. Bank Sakhis and the Financial Inclusion cadres help with the process.
9. **Livelihood support:** Members take up farm or non-farm activities with support from livelihood cadres, training, producer groups, RSETIs, SVEP and Lakhpati Didi initiatives.

## 7. Documents Typically Required

There is no single published document list for "joining NRLM", because an SHG is a voluntary group and most documents are needed only at the bank-linkage and training stages. Based on RBI/bank guidelines for SHG-bank linkage and the MSRLS programme rules, the following are generally needed:

**To open an SHG savings bank account**
- Account opening form
- **Resolution of the SHG** authorising the account and naming the authorised office bearers
- **KYC documents of the office bearers** (identity and address proof — Aadhaar, passport, driving licence, etc.). If the bank does e-KYC, the Aadhaar card alone is enough.
- PAN is not required to open the SHG's account; Form 60 is used instead, where needed.
- KYC of **all** SHG members is not required to open the SHG account; KYC of office bearers suffices.

**To apply for an SHG bank loan** (as listed by banks)
- Duly filled and signed loan application
- Resolution / loan authorisation letter from the group members
- Inter-se agreement executed by all members
- SHG records: savings register, loan register, minutes register, internal lending register, attendance register, passbooks and cash book
- Micro-credit plan prepared by the SHG and appraised by the federation/support agency (typically from the third linkage onward)
- Recommendation from the SRLM/mission staff
- KYC documents of the members and passport-size photographs
- A "no dues" / no-objection certificate is **not** required, and not all members need a personal savings account at the time of credit linkage.

**For individual members joining an SHG**
- MSRLS does not publish a fixed list. In practice, members are expected to have Aadhaar and proof of residence in the village, and a bank account (or to be helped to open one) for savings and insurance. This part should be confirmed with the local BMMU.

**For skills training (Aajeevika Skills/ASDP) or RSETI training**
- Proof of age (18–35 for ASDP), identity proof, and proof of belonging to a rural poor household (PIP listing, BPL status, or Gram Panchayat certificate). Exact requirements are set by each training provider.

Because document requirements differ by bank and by programme, applicants should confirm the current list with their **BMMU/C&RD Block office** or the concerned bank branch before applying.

## 8. Financial Assistance Structure

NRLM provides money to **community institutions**, not to individuals.

| Component | Who receives it | Amount / rule (as per MSRLS FAQ unless noted) |
|---|---|---|
| **Start-up Fund** | SHGs (and VOs/CLFs) | A small start-up grant; the MSRLS success stories mention ₹2,500 per SHG |
| **Revolving Fund (RF)** | SHGs that are at least 3/6 months old, follow Panchasutra, and have not received RF before | ₹10,000 minimum up to ₹15,000 per SHG, as corpus to strengthen financial management and build credit history |
| **Vulnerability Reduction Fund (VRF)** | Village-level federations (VOs) in intensive blocks | About ₹1,500 per member, to address food security, health security and needs of vulnerable persons |
| **Community Investment Support Fund (CIF)** | CLFs in intensive blocks; held in perpetuity and used to lend to SHGs or fund collective activities | About ₹3,000 per SHG member to the CLF; until a CLF exists, up to ₹50,000 to an SHG directly against a micro-plan. The MSRLS 2022–23 report mentions a plan to give CIF at ₹1,10,000 per SHG in the CLF-based model for FY 2023–24 |
| **Interest subvention** | Women SHGs | Covers the difference between the bank's lending rate and 7% on bank credit, up to ₹3 lakh per SHG as stated in the MSRLS FAQ; national rules have since been extended for higher loan sizes |
| **Bank loans** | SHGs | Collateral-free for loans up to ₹20 lakh per SHG under RBI guidelines; a Lakhpati Didi provision allows loans of up to ₹1 lakh to one woman per SHG |

**Meghalaya progress (cumulative to March 2023, MSRLS annual report 2022–23):**
- 12 districts, 46 blocks, about 6,497 villages covered
- About **4.27 lakh households** mobilised into **44,037 SHGs**, **4,598 VOs** and **68 CLFs** (the report is not always consistent in its figures; for example, 43,248 SHGs appears elsewhere in the text)
- Revolving Fund: about ₹58.8 crore to 39,143 SHGs
- CIF to VOs: about ₹169.8 crore to 4,097 VOs
- VRF: about ₹10.1 crore to 858 VOs
- Total community funds released to SHGs, VOs and CLFs: about **₹288 crore**
- Bank credit: about ₹211 crore accessed by 18,943 SHGs

**Latest reported position (Chief Minister, February 2026):** SHGs cover nearly 90% of households; over 58,000 groups have access to roughly ₹1,100 crore, about 70% of it bank credit; and 41,323 SHG members have achieved "Lakhpati" status.

Figures change every year, so current numbers should be confirmed with MSRLS or the latest government announcement.

## 9. Components and Related Programmes under NRLM in Meghalaya

- **Farm livelihoods / MKSP (Mahila Kisan Sashaktikaran Pariyojana):** Agro-ecological practices, agri-nutri gardens, livestock support through Pashu Sakhis, Krishi Sakhis, organic farming clusters, seed banks and custom hiring centres.
- **Non-farm livelihoods:** Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme (SVEP), enterprise promotion, PMFME linkages, rural haats and marts, market linkage for SHG products (including the MegSaras store catalogue), group enterprises in partnership with industry and NGOs.
- **Lakhpati Didi initiative:** Helps SHG women take up multiple livelihoods and earn at least ₹1 lakh a year on a sustainable basis (around ₹10,000 a month).
- **RSETI (Rural Self Employment Training Institutes):** Bank-run training institutes that train rural youth for self-employment and link them to bank loans. Meghalaya has five: SBI (Ri Bhoi/Umran), SBI Tura, Meghalaya Rural Bank Nongstoin, PNB Mawphlang and MCAB Williamnagar.
- **Aajeevika Skills (ASDP) / skills and placement:** Training and placement for rural poor youth aged 18–35 through project implementing agencies.
- **Financial inclusion:** Bank Sakhis (SHG members trained as business correspondents), Bima Sakhis for insurance, financial literacy training, Saksham centres at CLF offices.
- **Social development layering:** Social inclusion (special SHGs for elderly, persons with disabilities, single women), FNHW (nutrition and health), Gender, and PRI–CBO convergence including Village Poverty Reduction Plans (VPRP).
- **Aajeevika Grameen Express Yojana (AGEY):** Support for community-run rural transport vehicles; MSRLS received vehicles flagged off by the Chief Minister.

## 10. Institutional Structure

- **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD), Government of India:** Designs and funds the mission (90:10 with Meghalaya as a special-category state).
- **National Rural Livelihoods Promotion Society (NRLPS) / National Mission Management Unit:** Technical support agency at the national level.
- **Government of Meghalaya — Community & Rural Development (C&RD) Department:** The administrative department for MSRLS.
- **Meghalaya State Rural Livelihoods Society (MSRLS):** The state nodal agency, headed by a Chief Executive Officer assisted by a Chief Operating Officer, State Mission Manager, State Project Managers and support staff. Located at Monfort Building, Dhankheti, Law College Road, Shillong.
- **District Mission Management Units (DMMU) and Block Mission Management Units (BMMU):** Implement the programme at district and block level.
- **Community institutions:** SHGs, Village Organisations, Cluster Level Federations, along with community cadres.
- **Banks and partners:** Bank branches (credit linkage), RSETIs, NABARD, SIDBI, line departments, National Resource Organisations and technical partners.
